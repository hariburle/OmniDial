Getting Started
with OmniDial

OmniDial replaces your phone's default dialer with one that handles regular calls and WhatsApp calls, learns how you like to reach each person, and automates the boring stuff. This guide takes you from install to power user.

Install & set OmniDial as your default dialer

  1. Download the latest APK from the Releases section and install it (your phone may ask you to allow installs from your browser — that's normal for apps installed outside the Play Store).
  2. Open OmniDial. It will offer to become your default phone app — accept. This is what lets it handle incoming calls, outgoing calls, and your call history like the dialer that came with your phone.
  3. That's it for the basics — you can already make and receive calls. The next steps unlock everything else.
2

Allow the permissions it asks for

OmniDial asks for each permission with a reason. Here's the plain-English version:

  • Contacts — to show your address book, nicknames, and starred contacts.
  • Phone — to place and manage calls as your dialer.
  • Call log — to show your recent calls with SIM badges and durations.
  • SMS — only used if you set up automation rules that send a text (like a gate-buzzer confirmation).
Privacy note: everything — contacts, call logs, rules, favorites — stays on your phone. OmniDial has no account, no cloud sync, and no ads.

4

Make your first calls

Dial with T9 search

On the Keypad tab, just start typing a name on the number keys — 668 finds "Mot…" (M=6, O=6, T=8). Matching contacts, including ones you saved under a nickname, appear instantly above the keypad. Tap to call.

Redial in one tap

The row of avatars above the keypad is your quick recents bar — tap any face to call them back immediately.

Silence a ringing phone gracefully

When a call comes in, just pick the phone up, tap the screen, or press a volume button — the ringer quiets down while the call keeps ringing, so you get a moment to decide.

5

Star your VIPs

Your Favorites tab is a speed-dial grid for the people you call most. To add someone:

  1. Find them in Contacts or Recents, open their details, and tap the ⭐ star at the top.
  2. They appear on your Favorites grid with one-tap call buttons.
🫳 Arrange it your way

Tap the checkmark in the top-right of the Favorites tab, then drag cards anywhere — even across rows. Your layout is saved and survives reinstalls. You can also pick between Bento, Grid, and Material card styles in Settings.

If a contact has several numbers, pick which one is the default when you star them — that's the number the favorite will dial.

6

Phone or WhatsApp? Teach it once

OmniDial's signature trick: for each contact, it remembers whether you prefer a regular call or a WhatsApp call — and then just does the right thing.

  • Let it learn: the first time you call someone, it asks "Phone or WhatsApp?" and remembers your answer.
  • Set it directly: open the contact's details and use the Preferred Channel row — no call needed.
  • Change your mind: tap the ✕ next to the preference to go back to being asked each time.
Try this: set your family to WhatsApp and your office contacts to Phone. After a week, your Favorites cards collapse to a single button each — the right one, every time.
7

Everyday speed tricks

  • Speed dial: assign numbers 2–9 in Settings, then long-press a key on the keypad to call instantly.
  • Nicknames: give contacts nicknames ("Mom", "Plumber") — T9 search finds them, and the Nicknames filter tab surfaces them all in one place.
  • Multi-number contacts: tap a contact with several numbers to choose which to call; set a default so the favorite always dials the right one.
  • Post-call notes: after hanging up, add a timestamped note to the call record ("call back Thursday") or set a callback reminder right from the quick-action card.
  • Missed calls: tap a missed-call notification to jump straight to that entry in Recents, highlighted for you.
8

Power features: rules, spam & dual SIM

🤖 Automation rules (Rules tab)

Rules react to incoming calls automatically. Start with the Recipes gallery — ready-made templates like:

  • Gate buzzer: auto-answers your building's intercom, sends the open code, and hangs up.
  • Delivery gate, office extension, VIP ring — and you can build your own sequences (ring → delay → answer → speaker → hang up) with a dry-run simulator to test safely.

🛡️ Spam protection

Open the spam center to block numbers manually or switch on auto-block presets (top spammers, private numbers, foreign prefixes). Incoming spam shows a red Spam Risk badge; verified businesses and deliveries get green and amber badges.

📡 Dual SIM

With two SIMs, choose a global mode in Settings — Ask & Learn (per-contact SIM preferences), System (just follow your phone's default), or International (ask for foreign numbers). You can also pin a preferred SIM to any individual number, and OmniDial warns you before routing a call through a roaming SIM.

9

Back up your setup

Once you've arranged favorites, trained channel preferences, and built rules, protect that work: open Settings → Backup and tap Backup Now. It saves everything — favorites, rules, speed dials, SIM preferences, spam filters — to your Documents/OmniDial/ folder, where it survives even an uninstall. Restoring on a new phone is one tap.

10

Pro tips for the best experience

  • Spend 10 minutes on favorites. Star your top 8–12 contacts and arrange them — that's 90% of the app's daily value.
  • Train channels deliberately. The first week, answer the "Phone or WhatsApp?" prompt thoughtfully instead of dismissing it. The payoff is one-tap correct calling forever after.
  • Use nicknames for hard-to-spell names. T9 shines when contacts have short nicknames.
  • Try one recipe. Even if you never build a custom rule, the gate-buzzer recipe alone is worth opening the Rules tab once.
  • Back up before big changes. Reorganizing favorites or experimenting with rules? One-tap backup first — restore is just as fast.
  • Keep it updated. Grab the newest APK from the Releases page; each version's notes say exactly what changed.
Coming from another dialer? Your contacts, call history, and system favorites import automatically on first launch — nothing to migrate by hand.
